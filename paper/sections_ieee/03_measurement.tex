\section{The Measurement Layer}
\label{sec:measurement}

The measurement layer turns video into positions and positions into the objects the models use.
It has no learned parameters beyond the per-game detectors, and every step is validated against Riot data.

\subsection{From broadcast to game coordinates}

\paragraph*{Clock alignment}
We crop the broadcast's game clock at 10\,fps and read it with a digit classifier trained on one game, labelled by a single hand-checked anchor reading and the clock's own progression.
Each clock tick yields one pair (video time, game time); a robust fit over the roughly one thousand ticks of a game gives the offset of each live segment and separates live play from replays, which show earlier times for a few seconds and then return.
All \V{clockGames} videos align (Fig.~\ref{fig:measurement}a); the median share of frames carrying a live game time is \V{clockLiveMedPct}\%, the median misread rate \V{clockMisreadMedPct}\%, and the last live reading falls a median \V{clockEndDiffMed}\,s before the official game end, the length of the closing camera sequence.
\V{clockReplaySeg} replay segments are detected and excluded.

\paragraph*{Minimap registration}
The minimap is an orthographic projection, so four numbers (scale and offset per axis) map pixels to map coordinates.
The per-pixel median of a game's minimap frames removes the moving icons and leaves the static map; we register it to the walkable mask of the navigation graph by maximising their correlation.
Two independent crops of the same game agree to within 0.04 pixels, and Riot's tower coordinates and per-minute player positions land on their icons.

\paragraph*{Tracking}
Each game has ten known champions, and the v5 timeline gives every champion's exact position once a minute.
We therefore train detectors per game: for each champion a linear discriminant $w=\Sigma^{-1}(\mu_{\mathrm{champ}}-\mu_{\mathrm{bg}})$, where $\Sigma$ is the covariance of random minimap patches, so static structures such as towers fall into the background distribution without labels.
Scores are calibrated to likelihood ratios.
A Viterbi pass per champion chooses among detection peaks, a `held' state (icon occluded, position kept) and a `dead' state that is allowed only inside death windows from the kill events; transitions are priced by geodesic distance against the maximum movement speed.

\paragraph*{Validation}
We validate on a harder task than the production run: detectors are trained and tracks pinned only at even minutes, and the tracker is scored at odd minutes, a full 60\,s from the nearest pin (production pins every minute, at most 30\,s away).
Over \V{trkN} champion-minutes the median error is \V{trkMed} units and \V{trkLtSix} of errors are below 600 units (Table~\ref{tab:tracker}, Fig.~\ref{fig:measurement}).
Detected positions (\V{trkDetShare}\% of all) have median error \V{trkDetMed} and \V{trkDetLtSix} below 600; interpolated positions (\V{trkInterpShare}\%) have median \V{trkInterpMed}.
Error grows with crowding: the share below 600 falls from \V{trkCrowdZeroLtSix} with no other champion within 2,000 units to \V{trkCrowdFourLtSix} with four or more.
The upper tail (P95 \V{trkPninetyfive}) comes from identity errors in crowds, which is why formation agreement with v5 is \V{formExact} exact and \V{formRand} by Rand index.
The analyses below are built to be robust to this error: contrasts are headlines only if their sign agrees at three pit radii, every V-layer finding has an R-layer counterpart or a sensitivity run without mostly-interpolated snapshots, and the LB-QRE fit is repeated without decisions that rest on held or interpolated positions.

\subsection{Objects defined by theory}

\paragraph*{Navigation graph}
We rasterise the map into 100-unit cells and call a cell walkable if a player stood on it in at least three of 1,994 archive games on the current map (2024--2026, base terrain); changing the threshold to two or five games moves median geodesic distances to the pits by 0--65 units.
The walkable cells form a graph with 16-neighbourhood moves (8-neighbourhoods overestimate distances by up to 8\%), and we precompute all pairwise geodesic distances.
Every distance in this paper is geodesic.

\paragraph*{Formations are set partitions}
Two alive teammates are \emph{together} if their geodesic distance is at most $r=1{,}900$ units, five seconds of movement at the median speed.
The connected components of this proximity graph partition the alive roles; with all five alive the formation is an element of the partition lattice $\Pi_5$ ordered by refinement.
$\Pi_5$ has \V{latticeN} elements in \V{latticeShapes} shapes, its Hasse diagram has \V{latticeEdges} covering edges and diameter \V{latticeDiam} (Fig.~\ref{fig:lattice}); all \V{latticeObservedAll} partitions occur in our data.
Increasing $r$ only merges blocks, so the radius sensitivity is a chain in the lattice.
Dead players are removed by restricting the partition to the alive set, never imputed.

\begin{lemma}[Lattice metric]\label{lem:metric}
For $\sigma,\tau\in\Pi_5$ let $d(\sigma,\tau)=|\sigma|+|\tau|-2|\sigma\vee\tau|$, where $|\cdot|$ counts blocks and $\vee$ is the join (finest common coarsening).
Then $d$ is a metric on $\Pi_5$, equals the shortest-path length in the Hasse diagram, and every shortest path from $\sigma$ to $\tau$ consists of exactly $|\sigma|-|\sigma\vee\tau|$ merges and $|\tau|-|\sigma\vee\tau|$ splits.
\end{lemma}
The metric is classical for upper semimodular lattices~\cite{monjardet1981metrics}; we also verify all three statements exhaustively on $\Pi_5$ (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).
It lets HASSE count merges and splits in Hasse steps, a unit that a reader can interpret.

\paragraph*{Control and readiness}
For a snapshot, each walkable cell belongs to the team whose nearest alive player reaches it first (a geodesic Voronoi split, Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}a); \emph{pit control} is the team's share of cells within the pit radius.
The \emph{$k$-arrival time} $\tau^{(k)}$ is the $k$-th order statistic of the team's geodesic travel times to the pit (Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}b): the earliest moment at which the team can have $k$ players there.
$k$ is the number of alive players within $R=1{,}500$ units of the pit centre (sensitivity $R\in\{1{,}000,2{,}000\}$).
Each player's \emph{zone} is one of pit, own pit side, opposite pit side, pit lane, mid, far side and base, defined relative to the pit and the team.
