\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{lem:metric}]
$\Pi_5$ ordered by refinement is a geometric, hence upper semimodular, lattice with rank $r(\pi)=5-|\pi|$.
For upper semimodular lattices $d(\sigma,\tau)=2r(\sigma\vee\tau)-r(\sigma)-r(\tau)$ is a metric equal to the shortest-path distance in the covering graph \citep{monjardet1981metrics}; substituting the rank gives the stated form.
Each covering step is one merge (rank $+1$) or one split (rank $-1$). On a path from $\sigma$ to $\tau$ with $M$ merges and $S$ splits, $M-S=r(\tau)-r(\sigma)$; on a shortest path also $M+S=d(\sigma,\tau)=2r(\sigma\vee\tau)-r(\sigma)-r(\tau)$. Hence $M=r(\sigma\vee\tau)-r(\sigma)=|\sigma|-|\sigma\vee\tau|$ and $S=|\tau|-|\sigma\vee\tau|$.
We verified all three statements exhaustively: breadth-first search on the Hasse diagram of the \V{latticeN} partitions (\V{latticeEdges} covering edges) reproduces $d$ for all pairs, and the triangle inequality holds for all triples.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{prop:threshold}]
Contesting yields expected win probability $pW(\eta+G)+(1-p)W(\eta-L)$, conceding yields $W(\eta-b+c)$.
Their difference is $W(\eta-L)+pS-W(\eta-b+c)=S(p-\pi^*)$ with $S$ and $\pi^*$ as stated.
Under logit choice with precision $\lambda$ the probability of the first option is $\Lambda(\lambda\cdot\text{difference})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{prop:unique}]
With $\nu=1$, $W_B=1-W_A$ implies that every outcome changes $B$'s win probability by minus $A$'s change, so $U_B=-U_A^\top$ in win-probability units, and the remaining terms ($\kappa\tau$, $\psi T_k$, $\omega$) depend only on a team's own action; write them $c_A^\top x$ and $c_B^\top y$ for mixed strategies $x,y$.
Team $A$'s logit best response to $y$ maximises $\lambda_A x^\top Uy-c_A^\top x+H(x)$, team $B$'s maximises $-\lambda_B x^\top Uy-c_B^\top y+H(y)$.
Dividing by $\lambda_A$ and $\lambda_B$, both are the first-order conditions of the saddle problem $\max_x\min_y F(x,y)$ with $F$ as stated.
$F$ is continuous, strictly concave in $x$ and strictly convex in $y$ on compact convex sets, so a saddle point exists (minimax theorem) and is unique by strictness; every logit QRE is such a saddle point and conversely.
\end{proof}

\section{HASSE: Estimation}
\label{app:hasse}

\paragraph*{Likelihood} For setup $e$ with pattern sequence $z_{1:T}$, the complete-data likelihood is $w_{\mathrm{cls}(e),z_1}\prod_t P(z_t\mid z_{t-1})\prod_t P_{z_t}(\pi_t\mid\pi_{t-1})\prod_{B\in\pi_t}P_{z_t}(g_B\mid\pi_t)$; snapshots after the kill are missing and drop out of the product.
\paragraph*{E step} Exact forward--backward over $K$ states.
\paragraph*{M step} (i)~For each pattern and snapshot, the centre $\mu_{c,t}$ is chosen among the 52 partitions by an exact Viterbi pass that adds a smoothness penalty of one unit per Hasse step between consecutive centres. (ii)~$(\theta_c,\iota_c)$ maximise a concave exponential-family likelihood whose gradient is observed minus expected distance, bounded in $[0,20]$. (iii)~Zone parameters by penalised multinomial logit (Gaussian penalty 0.1). (iv)~$w$, $\xi$, $q$ in closed form.
\paragraph*{Initialisation and selection} Ten restarts per $K$, even seeds from $k$-medoids on the lattice path distance; held-out log-likelihood on five series-grouped folds; BIC and ICL on the full data. Recovery on synthetic data drawn from the model (three planted patterns, \V{synthN} setups, three snapshots, five restarts): all restarts reach the same log-likelihood (spread \V{synthLLspread}), \V{synthPathsOK} centre paths are recovered exactly, rigidity and inertia lie within \V{synthThetaErr} and \V{synthIotaErr} of the planted values, and the first-snapshot assignment has ARI \V{synthAri} with the truth (\V{synthAriMissing} when the last snapshot is missing in 40\% of setups).
\paragraph*{$H_{\mathrm{mono}}$} The Viterbi step for $\mu$ is restricted to transitions $\mu_{t}\leq\mu_{t+1}$ in the refinement order.

\section{LB-QRE: Specification}
\label{app:qre}

\paragraph*{Commitment time and actions} $t_c$ is the first at-risk snapshot in $[s,s{+}180]$ with three or more players of either team within $R$ of the pit, otherwise the last at-risk snapshot (objective taken next, or window end). $m$ counts alive players in zone `far' at $t_c$; $\tau=1$ if the team already had at least $k$ players at the pit at the spawn. Players farther than 60\,s of movement at the spawn are infeasible for the pit.
\paragraph*{State values} For each contest, values for the objective (to either team), a fight won or lost by two kills, and one tower are read from the event regressions at the out-of-fold $W$ of the last v5 frame at least 30\,s before the spawn; dragons resolve to the gainer's next drake index.
\paragraph*{Estimation} Nested pseudo-likelihood alternates a conditional-logit maximisation in $\theta$ (analytic gradient) with a strategy update; team deviations are shrunk towards global values with prior standard deviations 1.0 for $\log\lambda$, $\kappa$, $\psi$ and 0.5 for $\log\nu$, $\omega_k$, $\omega_m$.
Teams with too few contests share a pooled `other' parameter.

\section{T1 and Faker}
\label{app:t1}

T1 contributes \V{tOneSetups} setups, the most of any team, and won Worlds~2025.
Every analysis here was specified before it was run, compares T1 or Faker with the rest of the league on the same measures, and reports intervals; null results are reported as such.

\paragraph*{A1 Pattern fingerprint}
T1's usage over the six patterns is \V{tOneUsage} (\Cref{tab:usage}).
Its nearest team is \V{tOneNearest} (total-variation distance \V{tOneNearestTV}; nearest in \V{tOneNearestP}\% of resamples), then \V{tOneSecond} and \V{tOneThird}.
Given the low split-half reliability of all fingerprints (\Cref{sec:fingerprints}), this places T1 inside the league's main cluster rather than apart from it.

\paragraph*{A2 Decision parameters}
T1's precision is $\lambda=\V{lamTOne}$ \V{lamTOneCI} and its precision on the objective $\lambda\nu=\V{lamObjTOne}$ \V{lamObjTOneCI}; neither separates T1 from Gen.G ($\lambda=\V{lamGenG}$ \V{lamGenGCI}) or from the league (\Cref{fig:teamparams}, \Cref{tab:teamparams}).

\paragraph*{A3 Faker in the setup}
Against other starting mid laners ($n=\V{fakerN}$ vs \V{fakerOthersN} setups), Faker is closer to the pit before the spawn by \V{fakerDpit} \V{fakerDpitCI} units and arrives earlier in his team's order (rank \V{fakerRank} \V{fakerRankCI}); he is no more often alone, in the main block or central in the team's spanning tree (\Cref{tab:faker}, \Cref{fig:t1}a).

`T1 does not panic when behind' describes the whole league as well as T1.
